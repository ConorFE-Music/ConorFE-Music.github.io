Orchestral / 2026

The Final Sovereign

An epic video game boss track I composed, arranged, and produced, capturing the scene of an important, intense, and emotional fight.

ARTIST STATEMENT

For my composition “The Final Sovereign”, I attempted to put the knowledge I had accumulated through studying my favourite video game composers into a single track. I approached this piece as the soundtrack to an important battle or encounter, inspired by the Dark Souls trilogy and the compositions of Yuka Kitamura. I originally began by experimenting with the final climax of the piece. I created a motif for the french horns, and built it around tense, epic string ostinatos, a choir, and other orchestral elements.I wanted this climactic 16 bar passage to tell a small story through the progression. The first eight bars represent an intense battle, one of which’s result is indeterminable during the current moment. The following four bars provide a temporary triumph, implying that perhaps the main character has overcome this battle, but the final four bars completely shift away from that, undermining that victory by introducing an ominous harmonic movement, telling the listener that something is wrong, and this conflict is far from over. I also used orchestration to reinforce this climactic energy. I used the piatti to accentuate offbeats and downbeats, adding additional weight to the French horn motif. I avoided using them too frequently as I felt that excessive cymbal accents end up diminishing their impact and adding muddiness to the other orchestral sections. During this climax, I also created a tense string ostinato that contributes heavily to the epic character of this 16 bar passage. The progression makes heavy use of diminished chords and begins in E minor. The first eight bars portray a dark and tense E-minor character, making use of harmonic minor material and the tension that is created through the diminished harmony. During the following four bars, I shift the tonal center toward F# minor. I emphasize the relative major, A major, within this new tonal center. This gives the section a more triumphant and heroic vibe while still making use of F# minor’s harmonic language. In the final four bars, I introduce a chromatic shift outside of F# minor, most notably F natural, which pushes the listener away from the triumphant feeling, and creates a heavily ominous harmonic colour. The process in which I composed this piece was different from my usual style, as I started with the climax rather than either building towards it naturally, finding a chord progression I like, or just starting with a motif I like and then building towards that. Once I finished up the climax however, I struggled to find a buildup that felt satisfying to me. I eventually left the piece for around a month, before finding the inspiration to return to it. I spent several days developing the rest of the composition, starting the track off with a somber buildup, featuring a violin lead with a Sul Tasto technique, using that soft, airy feel to begin, on top of tremolo string crescendos, vocals, and bass. These elements slowly bring the track to the climax I had already established. Before reaching the climax, I pull the energy back and create a false sense of security. I use a section that's much softer and heavily reverberated, with a piano subtly introducing the upcoming french horn motif. After this section, I introduce the main string ostinato of the climax, creating an epic 16 bar build up, leading into the main 16 bar climax. Following the climax, the composition continues with a different, less erratic string ostinato, while retaining the piatti accents on both downbeats and selected offbeats. I also introduce choir stabs every other bar to add variation and further develop the progression established during the climax. The piece begins to slow down, I shift the tonal center back to E minor and begin one final buildup. This section introduces a couple new qualities, in powerful low-brass stabs using tuba and trombones, and variation of the role of the church choir. Rather than having the entire choir changing chords every bar as it was in the previous climax, I have the sopranos and altos mirror the french horn motif, while the tenors and basses continue the main progression. This created more interaction between the melody and harmony, giving the arrangement more energy. The composition concludes with one final climax, combining that new choir variation, low-brass stabs, and also introducing trumpet accents before gradually fading out. My goal with this piece in general was to put everything I had been studying to the test, and to create a story throughout the harmony, orchestration, and trying to implement interesting changes in texture and tonal centers. As I further my knowledge, I find myself excited to push myself and improve my knowledge and creativity with music, and this track felt special because It felt as though I was proving to myself that I am capable of achieving my dreams, and capable of creating music that, without a single word, and tell a whole story.
